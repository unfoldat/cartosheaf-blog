# Verdict

**False** — as stated, the claim does not match Korean accessibility regulation.

## Reasoning

Two primary statutes were located and read in full text: the enforcement rule (시행규칙) under the Act on the Guarantee of Promotion of Convenience of Persons with Disabilities, the Aged, Pregnant Women, etc. (장애인·노인·임산부 등의 편의증진 보장에 관한 법률), and the enforcement rule under the Pedestrian Safety and Convenience Promotion Act (보행안전 및 편의증진에 관한 법률). Between them, these are the two statutes that actually govern tactile paving (점자블록) installation in Korea.

Neither contains a requirement for "30cm of unobstructed space on both sides of tactile paving." The only 30cm (0.3m) figures that exist in this area of law are:

- the minimum physical size of a single tactile block unit (0.3m × 0.3m), and
- the distance a warning (점형) block must be installed *in front of* a hazard, turning point, doorway, or facility — a spacing rule in the direction of travel, not a lateral buffer.

A genuine "no obstacles within a fixed distance on both sides of the block" concept does exist in real Korean accessibility practice — but the figure attached to it by the two disability-organization sources found (Korea Blind Union's installation appendix, and the 2025 article from the affiliated Support Center for Convenience Facilities for the Visually Impaired) is **60cm, not 30cm**, and both sources describe it explicitly as their own recommendation, not a quotation of binding law. The second source is unusually candid that it derived the 60cm figure itself, by combining the statute's 30cm pre-hazard offset with an unrelated bollard-spacing rule.

This gives a specific, evidenced account of how the tested claim likely went wrong: a real 30cm figure exists in Korean law, but it measures something else entirely, and the number that actually describes a both-sides clearance zone in Korean practice is double that, and is guidance rather than regulation.

## Confidence and open gaps

This verdict is supported by direct reading of primary legal text, which is the strongest evidence available, and corroborated independently by two disability-organization sources. It is not being called "Unsupported" because there is enough evidence to affirmatively state what the law says, rather than only an absence of support for the claim.

Confidence is not total. Three sources that could plausibly contain a different, authoritative clearance figure could not be retrieved in this research pass: a 국토교통부 (Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport) road-safety-facility administrative guideline, the Seoul Facilities Corporation's detailed sidewalk design standard drawings, and the full text of KS F 4561 (the national tactile-block product standard). None of the evidence gathered suggests any of these would introduce a validated "30cm both sides" figure — the pattern across every source found is 30cm for block size/pre-hazard offset and 60cm for lateral clearance — but their absence is a genuine limitation of this fact-check and is called out in the Editorial Recommendation rather than glossed over.
